\section*{Background and motivation}

Multiome RNA-velocity methods emit several per-gene quantities, each offered as a biological readout: a transcription rate $\alpha$, a degradation rate $\gamma$, and a chromatin-to-transcription lag offered as a clock for regulatory timing. A derived quantity is usable as a feature only if it is reliable, and reliability is testable: it should reproduce when a different algorithm is run on the \emph{same} cells, hold up in independent data, and agree with an external measurement of the same rate where one exists --- axes rarely applied to individual velocity outputs. Recent general benchmarks score velocity at the embedding and transition-vector level and report method-dependent direction [25,26,27], but neither sort the per-gene outputs by reliability nor anchor them to an external measurement. We ask not ``which method wins'' but how to measure the reliability of a velocity output, and build a map telling an analyst which outputs to trust and which need orthogonal validation.

\section*{Methods: a four-axis reliability protocol}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig07_reliability_map_v2.png}
\caption{Velocity-output reliability map. Rows are per-gene outputs; columns are reproducibility evidence (cross-method, cross-dataset), accuracy evidence (comparison with a measured rate, and whether it exceeds abundance) and the routing decision (use; use only beside an abundance baseline; do not use without an independent measurement).}
\label{fig2}
\end{figure*}

We ran up to five velocity arms --- an RNA-only scVelo floor [1,9] plus four chromatin-informed methods (MultiVelo [3], MultiVeloVAE [4], MoFlow [5], CRAK-Velo [6]) --- on human hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (10x Multiome, GSE209878; day0+day7 integrated, 21,878 cells), branching from a common preprocessing. The methods do not estimate the same lag: MultiVelo's priming interval (t\_sw1; coupled-on duration t\_sw2 $-$ t\_sw1 as sensitivity), MultiVeloVAE's rate proxy 1/$\alpha_c$ $-$ 1/$\alpha$, and dynamic-time-warping offsets for MoFlow and CRAK-Velo. Each per-gene output was tested on four axes. \textbf{(1) Cross-method reproducibility:} rank agreement on one method pair and one shared gene set, under every lag definition, with a gene-label permutation-FDR test (N=10$^4$). \textbf{(2) Within-lineage ATAC shuffle:} within each lineage we permuted ATAC profiles across cells, which destroys cell-level chromatin--RNA pairing while preserving lineage-level chromatin distributions, and re-fit. \textbf{(3) Cross-dataset replication} in five external multiomes spanning tissue distance (human fetal cortex, E18 mouse brain, same-tissue human BMMC, HSPC-derived macrophages, mouse gastrulation), the last against six predictions sealed before fitting. \textbf{(4) External anchoring:} fitted $\alpha$ against measured synthesis rates (K562 TT-seq; a second source as replication) and fitted $\gamma$ against measured mRNA half-life (SLAM-seq), with transcript abundance as a competing baseline. CIs are paired gene bootstraps conditional on the fits, checked against a co-expression-module bootstrap.

\section*{Results}

\textbf{Among the per-gene outputs, $\alpha$ reproduces across methods; the lag does not, under any definition.} $\alpha$ reproduced at Spearman $\rho$=+0.882 [+0.856, +0.904] (MultiVelo vs MultiVeloVAE, 538 genes), and the RNA-only floor recovered it too (+0.82 to +0.89). On the same genes the lag reached +0.364 [+0.285, +0.440] with MultiVelo's priming interval and +0.163 [+0.078, +0.247] with the coupled-on duration; computing both methods' lags from one identical formula did not raise agreement (signed +0.124, magnitude $-$0.009). Against MoFlow the lag did not reproduce (priming +0.038 [$-$0.046, +0.122]). Every $\alpha$-minus-lag contrast excluded zero (smallest $\Delta$$\rho$ +0.518 [+0.441, +0.596]), also under the module bootstrap (Figure 1). Direction stayed near balance (sign agreement 54.6\%, n=560; chromatin-leads fractions 41--49\%), so a genome-wide ``chromatin primes transcription'' ordering is not supported. Within MultiVelo the coupled-on duration is moderately stable across refits on 12 overlapping 70\% subsamples (+0.624, $\alpha$ +0.949; the priming interval was not refit-tested), so the failure is reproducibility across methods and definitions, not necessarily within-method noise. $\gamma$ was fragile ($\rho$$\approx$$-$0.11) and did not recover a measured half-life (RNA-only scVelo ran reversed, $-$0.224).

\textbf{The lag survives destruction of cell-level chromatin pairing (axis 2).} Shuffling ATAC across cells within lineage left the MultiVelo priming-interval distribution statistically unchanged (Mann--Whitney p=0.65, KS p=0.46), preserved its per-gene ranking ($\rho$=0.75; 0.72 for the coupled-on duration) and left the chromatin likelihood unchanged (0.239$\rightarrow$0.237). The fitted lag therefore depends little on which cell's chromatin is paired with which cell's RNA. Because the shuffle preserves lineage-level chromatin, this does not show that chromatin contributes nothing overall; the cell$\times$gene velocity matrix, a separate target, shows a small bounded chromatin contribution.

\textbf{The $\alpha$-over-lag ordering replicates, including under preregistration (axis 3).} On the same genes and method pair, $\alpha$ exceeded the lag in HSPC-derived macrophages ($\Delta$$\rho$ +0.843 [+0.773, +0.912]) and mouse gastrulation (+0.979 [+0.916, +1.041]). Cross-dataset $\alpha$ decayed with tissue distance yet stayed reproducible (+0.643 to +0.32) while the lag stayed low (+0.03 to +0.19; method pairs differ by dataset). Mouse gastrulation, the only preregistered system, passed six-of-six predictions sealed before fitting; HSPC and the other systems were not preregistered.

\textbf{$\alpha$ is reproducible beyond abundance, but its kinetic accuracy is not established (axis 4).} Fitted $\alpha$ tracked the measured TT-seq synthesis rate (non-housekeeping $\rho$ +0.24 to +0.29), but the second synthesis source was null, and transcript abundance tracked the first at least as strongly (+0.410 versus +0.262). After removing mean expression from both methods' $\alpha$, cross-method agreement remained high (partial Spearman +0.715 [+0.659, +0.765], from +0.882) while the lag stayed low (+0.182). $\alpha$ therefore carries a method-independent component beyond expression level, but that component has not been shown to be kinetic; it may come from the unspliced and spliced counts both methods read.

\textbf{Profile likelihood gives relative precision, not a coordinate-free identifiability claim.} In log coordinates on MultiVelo's objective, $\alpha$ is located more tightly relative to its own magnitude than the lag (median curvature ratio 3.53, $\alpha$ tighter in 95\% of 258 interior-lag genes; 2.49 with nuisance parameters freed, on the 148 genes with positive curvature on both axes). The comparison depends on coordinates: expressing the lag as a fraction of the pseudotime horizon reverses the ratio (0.10), and neither choice is canonical. Most boundary-pinned lags (199 of 205) come from induction-only fits, reflecting trajectory coverage more than non-identifiability. We therefore claim cross-method non-reproducibility, not strict non-identifiability; ConsensusVelo's weakly identified switch times [41] are consistent with this reading but do not prove it.

\section*{Conclusion}

We distil these axes into a velocity-output \textbf{reliability map} that keeps two kinds of evidence apart (Figure 2): \emph{reproducibility} --- do two reasonable methods, run on the same cells, rank genes the same way? --- and \emph{accuracy} --- has the number been compared with a laboratory measurement of the same rate, and with what result? $\alpha$ is the only per-gene output that is reproducible, including beyond abundance; its accuracy beyond abundance is not shown. Two further outputs are usable only as population statements (the near-50/50 directional balance and the direction agreement of canonical priming markers, which is correlational). The chromatin-opening rate $\alpha_c$ ($\rho$=0.29), $\gamma$, and the per-gene lag magnitude, sign and timing under every definition tested are not reproducible. The routing rule follows: enter transcript abundance first, add $\alpha$ only as its abundance-orthogonal component and keep it only if held-out performance improves, and do not consume a single-method lag, sign or $\gamma$ without an orthogonal measurement. Baseline features predict $\alpha$ on held-out lineages ($\rho$=+0.31) but not the lag ($\rho$$\approx$0.05), and day-0 accessibility adds nothing beyond abundance ($\Delta$$\rho$ $-$0.02). Limits: pseudotime is not wall-clock; external replications are single-sample (no donor variation); the $\alpha$ anchor is one of two sources, measured in K562 rather than HSPC; within-method stability was assessed only for MultiVelo's coupled-on duration; and we audited per-gene parameters, not trajectories.
